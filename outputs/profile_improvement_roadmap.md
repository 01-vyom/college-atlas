# Profile Improvement Roadmap

This roadmap is aimed directly at the college list in the atlas. The goal is
not to add random activities. The goal is to make Avij read as a
materials/biomaterials researcher who can plausibly grow toward medicine,
neuroscience, surgery, or physician-scientist work.

## Core Positioning

Use this as the center of the application:

> I started with scaffolded giant vesicles and lipophilic drug localization,
> then realized the core problem is biomaterials: how material structure,
> interfaces, and imaging can explain biological behavior and therapeutic
> delivery.

That story fits:

- Elite biomaterials reaches: Stanford, MIT, Northwestern, Berkeley.
- Materials-first engineering schools: Georgia Tech, Purdue, UIUC, UConn.
- California practical options: UC Merced, SJSU, UC Riverside, UC Davis, UCI.
- Medicine-facing research schools: Johns Hopkins, UCSD, UCLA, Case Western,
  Pitt, Duke, Vanderbilt, UAB, UIC.

## Next 3 Months

- Start one clinical or patient-adjacent track: hospital volunteer, clinic
  volunteer, hospice/senior care, community health, EMT observer route if
  age-eligible, or a health nonprofit. Treat consistency plus reflection as
  the target, not a fixed admissions threshold.
- Rewrite the ASDRP work as a translational abstract: problem, method, result,
  biological mechanism, patient relevance, and future clinical question.
- Add a computational layer: image quantification, statistics, reproducible
  figures, or a small dashboard for fluorescence localization.
- Build a target-college lab map with 2-3 labs or programs per school.
- If allowed, shadow 2-3 physicians, ideally one neuro/surgery/rehab/ER-adjacent
  physician. Keep private reflection notes and no patient details.

## Next 6 Months

- Submit or present the research outside the immediate lab: science fair, JSHS,
  school research night, ASDRP symposium, poster session, or credible student
  research venue.
- Create a public non-sensitive code or figure artifact.
- Build one sustained service role instead of several short one-offs.
- Prepare an ASDRP mentor letter packet: project abstract, methods used,
  obstacles solved, independence, and growth.
- Create a clinical/physician-scientist reading file: 10 papers/case studies on drug
  delivery, neural disease, tissue scaffolds, regenerative medicine, imaging, or
  surgical innovation.
- Add CPR/AED, Stop the Bleed, or first-aid training if feasible. Treat this as
  a supporting signal, not a substitute for service.

## Next 12 Months

- Work toward a meaningful combined clinical/service record if realistic, with
  one main sustained site; hour totals should support the story, not define it.
- Have one polished research artifact: poster PDF, abstract, manuscript draft,
  GitHub analysis, or conference presentation.
- Prepare three versions of the college story:
  - Biomaterials/engineering: Northwestern, MIT, Georgia Tech, Purdue, UIUC,
    Berkeley, UC Merced, SJSU, UConn.
  - Clinical/neuro/pre-med: Hopkins, UCLA, UCSD, UCI, UC Davis, Duke,
    Vanderbilt.
  - Accessible physician-scientist/clinical research: Pitt, UAB, UIC, Case
    Western, Wayne State.
- Build a recommender set: science teacher, ASDRP mentor, and clinical/service
  supervisor if the service role becomes substantial.
- Map every main activity to a competency: scientific inquiry, quantitative
  reasoning, teamwork, service, communication, reliability, and understanding
  others.

## Opportunity Ladder

Ranked by application value and feasibility:

| Priority | Opportunity | What to produce |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish a quantified ASDRP artifact | Poster PDF, abstract, figure panel, methods summary, and one quantified result. |
| 2 | Computational microscopy project | GitHub notebook, README, sample images or synthetic data, metrics, and limitations. |
| 3 | Sustained clinical or health-service role | Flexible hour target over time, supervisor, responsibilities, and reflection notes. |
| 4 | External research validation | Alameda County Science & Engineering Fair, CSEF/ISEF path if qualified, JSHS, symposium, poster night, student journal, or mentor-reviewed paper draft. |
| 5 | Lab leadership | Onboarding checklist, SOP, training log, or peer mentorship notes. |
| 6 | Science/health outreach | Workshop, explainer site, curriculum, participant count, feedback, and partner organization. |
| 7 | Target-college lab map | 2-3 labs or programs per target school, one paper/topic each, and a one-sentence fit. |
| 8 | Health-skills training | CPR/AED, Stop the Bleed, first aid, CNA if feasible, or EMT after age eligibility. In California/Alameda, EMT certification generally requires age 18. |

Avoid low-value substitutes: one-day shadowing with no reflection, vague
hospital volunteering with no responsibilities, generic research descriptions,
or unrelated club titles that do not add leadership, service, or technical
evidence.

## Concrete Opportunity Targets

- Alameda County Science & Engineering Fair, then CSEF/ISEF if qualified:
  best for external validation of ASDRP work.
- Regeneron STS senior year only if the research becomes substantially
  independent and paper-style.
- Computational biology extension using public NCBI GEO/SRA/BLAST resources:
  best for connecting CS, biology, and medicine without needing new wet-lab
  access.
- Hospital, hospice, clinic, elder-care, Red Cross, or community-health service:
  best for proving the medicine path is grounded in people.
- HOSA or Red Cross youth/service structure: useful if it produces leadership,
  service, or competition evidence rather than a passive membership line.
- CNA can be explored if time and age rules work; EMT is likely college-era or
  post-18 in California.

For any human-participant survey, patient-facing data, medical data, cell work,
or biohazard-adjacent project, check science-fair SRC/IRB rules before starting.
Do not retroactively seek approval after collecting data.

## UC Packaging

UC readers should see a coherent student, not a random collection of STEM and
pre-med interests.

Best PIQ set unless there is a genuine hardship story:

| PIQ | What to write |
|---|---|
| PIQ 6 academic subject | Biomaterials/cell biology: vesicles, lipophilic drugs, microscopy, and medicine. |
| PIQ 1 leadership | Training lab peers, writing a protocol, leading outreach, or organizing a research task. |
| PIQ 2 creativity | Experimental design, image noise/localization problems, or analysis code. |
| PIQ 7 community | Clinical volunteering, health outreach, or science teaching with concrete benefit to others. |

Backup:

- PIQ 4 if ASDRP was the major educational opportunity.
- PIQ 3 if microscopy/image analysis becomes the signature skill.

UC campus directions:

| Campus | Profile direction |
|---|---|
| UCLA | Research plus service plus clinical curiosity; apply engineering only if the engineering case is genuinely strong. |
| Berkeley | For BioE/MSE/Engineering, make math, physics, design, and research readiness obvious. |
| UCSD | Strongest story is bioengineering, neuro, biomaterials, drug delivery, and clinical research. |
| UCI | Strong match for biomedical research, clinical access, and individuality in PIQs. |
| UC Davis | Compassion, pre-health optionality, UC Davis Health access, and research presentation culture. |
| UC Riverside | Physician/community-health angle plus STEM readiness for CNAS/Bourns. |
| UCSB | Materials research curiosity; engineering requires a very strong engineering case. |
| UC Santa Cruz | Molecular biology, genomics, computational biology, and bioimage analysis. |
| UC Merced | Initiative, growth, MSE plus bioengineering, and practical California value. |

## Common App / Resume Packaging

Do not write the top activity as “Research intern.” Write it as a research
problem with methods and output.

Example activity line:

> Researcher, ASDRP: studied scaffolded giant vesicles and lipophilic
> localization using cell culture, fluorescence microscopy, and image analysis.

Example resume bullet:

> Designed and presented biomaterials research on scaffolded giant vesicles,
> connecting lipid localization, microscopy, and cell-facing material behavior.

Example essay thesis:

> My strongest theme is learning how engineered materials can make biological
> systems easier to observe, guide, and eventually heal.

## What Not To Do

- Do not abandon materials science just to look pre-med.
- Do not claim certainty about neurosurgery; frame it as curiosity about
  nervous-system repair, imaging, patient care, and surgical innovation.
- Do not collect disconnected shadowing hours without sustained service.
- Do not list lab techniques without explaining a question, result, or
  contribution.
- Do not apply to materials-first engineering schools without proving chemistry,
  physics, math, and engineering readiness.
